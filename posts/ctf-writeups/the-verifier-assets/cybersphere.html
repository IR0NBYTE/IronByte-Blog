THE NETWORK PROBLEM

The problem is not speed. It is reliability. (OpenSignal, State of Mobile Network Experience in Africa, 2024)

Reliability, not speed.

Speed is a red herring. A 200ms connection that drops mid-transaction is infinitely worse than a 2-second connection that completes. Africa has coverage. What it does not have is consistency. Connections drop without warning, mid-payment, mid-confirmation, mid-retry. The payment leaves the device but the confirmation never arrives. That is not a performance problem. That is an evidence problem.

Load shedding creates ghost transactions.

South Africa's load-shedding schedule regularly kills cellular base stations. When a base station goes down mid-transaction, the payment processes on the backend but the device never receives confirmation. The customer retries. The backend sees two requests and has no way to know if it is one transaction or two. This is well-documented, systematic, and happens every day.

The devices themselves are unreliable.

Low-end devices with unstable memory management and unreliable network hardware contribute to silent failures that no attestation API designed in a European or American lab will account for. The device drops a packet, corrupts a retry, or silently fails a TLS handshake, and nobody on the backend side knows it happened.

THE SCALE PROBLEM

This is a consumer market. The phone replaced the bank.

People do not have bank accounts. They have phones.

The success of mobile money (M-Pesa, MTN MoMo) is not accidental. The phone replaced the bank. Sometimes shared phones. That is the baseline. The smallest bank in this market has 5 million users. In Europe, a bank with 20,000 users can experiment. Here, there is no pilot. There is only production.

1% of incidents becomes a workforce problem that kills innovation.

5 million users, 1% encounter an anomaly: that is 50,000 support tickets. You cannot staff your way out of that. You cannot iterate. You stop shipping. This is the hidden cost of not having proper execution evidence at the device level. It is not a security problem. It is an operational problem that freezes your entire business.

Few players, massive impact.

A handful of banks serve the continent. 1% of the targetable population in South Africa alone is millions of people per year. (SARB, GSMA) The impact per breach is orders of magnitude larger than anywhere else. And the attackers know it. That is why they are moving here.

THE FRAGMENTATION PROBLEM

The real pain point that no lab environment will prepare you for.

We support 13,000+ device configurations in production. The market is far worse.

That number is not a market statistic. It is our production coverage. The actual market fragmentation is significantly larger. Low-end devices dominate: $30 phones with 2GB RAM, unpatched kernels from 2021, broken TEEs, unreliable storage. People share these devices. 5 users on one phone is normal. Manufacturers stop pushing patches after 6 months because there is no revenue in supporting a cheap handset.

Check any major vendor's documentation.

They will list a minimum supported Android version. Typically a recent one. Go look at Duo Security or any major mobile security vendor. Everything below their compatibility floor is out of scope. Now check what percentage of your fleet actually runs that version or higher. In most African deployments, the majority of devices fall below that line. They are not unsupported by accident. They are unsupported by design.

GODFATHER

THE OVERLAY ATTACK

When the victim opens their bank, they see a perfect fake. They never know.

1Victim installs a fake app from a phishing site or third-party store. It looks like a legitimate utility, VPN, or music player.
2The app requests Accessibility Service permissions. Most users tap "Allow" without reading. The malware now monitors everything on screen.
3Victim opens their real banking app. GodFather detects the launch through Accessibility Service.
4GodFather instantly displays a pixel-perfect HTML fake login screen on top of the real app. Same logo, same colors, same input fields.
5Victim enters credentials into the fake screen. GodFather captures username, password, PIN. Sends to attacker's C2 server.
6The fake overlay dismisses. The real app loads underneath. Victim never realizes they were phished.

What is Zygote?

Zygote is a special process that starts early during Android boot. It pre-loads ~15,000 framework classes, system resources, native libraries, and GPU drivers into memory. Then it sits and waits.

When any app needs to launch, Android does not start it from scratch. It tells Zygote to fork() itself. The new process inherits everything Zygote already loaded. This is how Android starts apps quickly.

Why this matters

Every single app on your device is a child of Zygote. The banking app. The payment SDK. The compliance tool. The RASP solution. All of them were forked from this one parent process.

If you compromise Zygote, every app launched after that point inherits your malicious code. Including the security tools meant to detect you.

THE ENTRY POINT

How a single Android setting write escalates to full device compromise.

1Android has a system setting called hidden_api_blacklist_exemptions. It controls which internal APIs are accessible. The system user (uid=1000) has write access to it. This is by design.
2When this setting changes, Android's ActivityManagerService reads the new value and sends it to Zygote over the Zygote socket. Zygote needs to know the current exemption list so all newly forked processes inherit the policy.
3The attacker writes a crafted payload into this setting. The payload embeds newline characters that inject additional arguments into the Zygote command stream. Specifically: --invoke-with, which tells Zygote to execute an arbitrary binary when forking.
4The attacker forces the Settings app to restart. When it relaunches, Zygote forks a new process, parses the poisoned arguments, and executes the attacker's payload as root (Zygote runs as root before dropping privileges).
5A reverse shell opens running as system user. The attacker can now impersonate any app, read any app's private data, and inject code into any process on the device.
!Any device with a security patch before June 2024 is vulnerable. That includes most POS terminals, low-end phones, and shared devices deployed across Africa right now.

WHY THIS BREAKS
EVERYTHING

Once Zygote is compromised, every security mechanism that runs in userland is blind:

  • Play Integrity runs after Zygote. The verdict says "clean" because the attestation runs inside the compromised process.
  • RASP / Runtime protection starts with the app. The app was forked from a compromised Zygote. The RASP is already running the attacker's code.
  • SSL Pinning relies on certificate stores on the device. With system access, replace the public cert with yours. You become trusted.
  • Overlay detection checks if accessibility services are doing something suspicious. But the detection runs inside the compromised process.

If someone can start before your app, you lose the game.
That is the fundamental problem.

THE COST OF NOT KNOWING

Ambiguity at scale destroys user experience and costs real money.

Ghost transactions

Customer taps. Payment processes. Network drops. POS retries. Backend sees two requests. Duplicate or new charge? Without device evidence, you cannot tell. 3-8% of cellular transactions in Africa.

90-day disputes

Issuer says payment happened. Acquirer says it did not. Neither can prove what happened on the device. 90-day process. Manual review. Someone absorbs the cost.

Whitelists that collapse at scale

You detect an accessibility service. Malware or screen reader for blind users? On 5 million users, the whitelist grows until it means nothing. Detecting "app X is enabled" is not enough. You need "app X is doing this, right now, to this field."

Observability is not evidence

"I see this, you see that." You cannot prove anything. The difference: with execution evidence, you can explain exactly what happened. Not what you think happened. What actually happened.

WHAT WOULD IT TAKE
TO ACTUALLY FIX THIS?

These are requirements the industry has not yet agreed on. Consider them.

Continuous, not snapshot. You cannot check at one moment and assume integrity. You need to observe every execution step, continuously, including the intervals between attestation checkpoints.

Deterministic signal, not heuristic. Risk scores are opinions. You need cryptographic proof that does not change if you come back 6 months later. Evidence, not inference.

Offline-capable by design. Your security cannot depend on a round-trip to a server on another continent. It needs to work on-device, with intermittent or no connectivity.

Works on the worst device in your fleet. Not the flagship. The $30 phone with Android 10 and a 2021 patch. That device handles real money too. If your solution does not cover it, you have no solution.
